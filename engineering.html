Featured Innovation / 01

Accessible Technology

Vibrant.

Haptic Headset

After interviewing members of the deaf community, I realized that many musical experiences still depend almost entirely on hearing.

I engineered a portable, wireless headset designed for deaf individuals that translates sound into physical haptic vibrations.

Rather than treating music as something that must only be heard, the device explores how rhythm, intensity, and changes in sound can also be physically experienced through touch.

Technical Development / 01

3D Modeling / Printing

Designing the physical structure of the headset required turning ideas into precise, testable geometry.

I used CAD and iterative 3D printing to develop the form, refine component placement, test fit, and improve the physical design through repeated prototypes.

01 CAD

3D Modeling

Developing the headset geometry, internal component layout, and mechanical structure before fabrication.

02 Fabrication

3D Printing

Producing physical iterations to test dimensions, ergonomics, assembly, and component fit.

Technical Development / 02

Electrical Design / Hardware

The headset required a compact electrical system capable of controlling the hardware responsible for physical haptic feedback.

I worked through component selection, wiring, power distribution, hardware integration, and fitting the electronics inside a wearable device.

01 Electronics

Electrical Design

Planning connections between power, control electronics, and the components responsible for generating haptic feedback.

02 Integration

Hardware

Integrating electronics into the physical headset while balancing size, reliability, portability, and accessibility.

Technical Development / 03

Programming

Software connects the incoming audio information to the physical response of the headset.

I developed the logic required to interpret input, control the electronic hardware, and translate sound into responsive patterns of vibration.

01 Logic

Signal Processing

Translating changing audio information into usable values that drive the headset's physical response.

02 Control

Hardware Control

Using software to coordinate the behaviour of the electronics and control the haptic feedback experienced by the user.
